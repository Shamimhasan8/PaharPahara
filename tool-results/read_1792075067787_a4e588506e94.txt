     7→     7→## 1 Purpose and final decision
     8→     8→
     9→     9→We will build a cloud-hosted web application that turns suspected hill disturbance, rainfall estimates, forecasts and official warnings into an inspection queue and a traceable assessment report. The Department of Environment is the intended primary user. Forest officers receive relevant evidence where forest boundaries and jurisdiction are verified; disaster-management staff use the preparedness view.
    10→    10→
    11→    11→The prototype must demonstrate the complete working loop: **collect source data, validate and store it, display the evidence, record a human decision, and generate a saved report**. A polished screen without these connections does not meet the build objective.
    12→    12→
    13→    13→The final architecture is **a Lovable-assisted React and TypeScript application on Vercel, the team's own Supabase project for database/authentication/storage and thin backend functions, and Python cloud workers in GitHub Actions with Earth Engine**. Development takes place in browser-based Codespaces. The deployed system must operate after the developer's computer and Codespace are closed.
    14→    14→
    15→    15→This document supersedes the earlier Technical Plan, Mentor 80/20 Plan and Cloud Build Plan for construction decisions. It incorporates the dossier and the supplied Round 2 audit, while recording unsupported or conflicting claims as verification tasks rather than implementation facts.

    17→    17→## 2 Prototype scope and users

    20→    20→
    21→    21→Lead the demonstration with the **8 July 2026 Chashma Hill case**. BSS and other reporting corroborate two child deaths in separate Chattogram landslides that day; do not use victim names or photographs. A retrospective case shows what evidence existed and when, not that the prototype would necessarily have prevented the event. [R1]
    22→    22→
    23→    23→Jangal Salimpur is an environmental study area, with jurisdiction marked unverified until authoritative records support it. Avoid assertions about residents, criminal activity or ownership. A real officer or volunteer is not required to operate the hackathon demo: provision reviewer test accounts and use archived evidence. Actual institutional adoption requires later agreement.

    56→    56→
    57→    57→### Data flow
    58→    58→
    59→    59→Supabase Cron creates due jobs and invokes thin dispatch/fetch functions. Workers and provider adapters validate inputs and write versioned records/files. The Vercel app reads permitted Supabase data. A reviewer records a decision, then requests a report job. The cloud worker creates an immutable PDF and the app retrieves an authorized download link.
    60→    60→
    61→    61→For the architecture submission, draw these connections explicitly: source adapters to jobs; jobs to database/storage; database to map/review; review to report job; BMD warnings to both the site and preparedness views. The enforcement case and preparedness decision remain separate records.

    63→    63→## 4 Sources and exact collection contracts
    64→    64→
    65→    65→### Satellite and terrain
    66→    66→
    67→    67→Use Earth Engine collections `COPERNICUS/S2_SR_HARMONIZED`, `GOOGLE/CLOUD_SCORE_PLUS/V1/S2_HARMONIZED`, `GOOGLE/DYNAMICWORLD/V1` and `COPERNICUS/DEM/GLO30_2024_1`. Initialize the Python API against one registered Earth Engine project, filter by verified site geometry and dates, and retain image/task identifiers. Check new imagery daily and process only new usable scenes. Load terrain once and retain its vintage. [R5–R8]
    68→    68→
    69→    69→Cloud Score+ replaces reliance on QA60 for historical pairs. Join corresponding assets; use `cs_cdf >= 0.60` as an initial development setting, then tune and freeze it. An absent quality asset is a missing input, not permission to keep every pixel. Dynamic World contributes tree/bare/built probabilities; it is derived from Sentinel-2 and is **corroborating evidence, not an independent sensor**.
    70→    70→
    71→    71→Mosaic the DEM's `DEM` band and set a suitable native/default projection before slope calculation. Preserve the approximately 30 m surface-model limitation. It cannot measure a new excavation's volume or current cliff geometry. RGB/NDVI bands can support 10 m products; indices using 20 m bands retain that effective limitation even when displayed on a finer grid.
    72→    72→
    73→    73→### Rainfall estimates
    74→    74→
    75→    75→Primary live source: **NASA IMERG Early V07**, short name `GPM_3IMERGHHE`. Resolve the collection through NASA CMR by short name/version rather than hard-coding the observed collection ID `C2723758340-GES_DISC`. Use authenticated Earthdata/`earthaccess` discovery and download; process only new granules and backfill gaps using a stored watermark. Check hourly initially, subject to measured worker quota. [R9]
    76→    76→
    77→    77→Inspect file metadata, field name, dimensions, units and fill values before parsing. A half-hour rainfall rate in mm/hour contributes rate × 0.5 hours; an already accumulated product must not be multiplied again. Reject missing/fill values. NASA PPS GeoTIFF accumulations are an alternative adapter only after registration and verification of their scale and time windows; never silently interchange them with HDF5 rates.
    78→    78→
    79→    79→IMERG's half-hour intervals are approximately 0.1 degrees, roughly 10 km, with nominal Early latency around four hours. Estimates are uncertain in mountainous terrain. Keep returned grid cells visible; two nearby hills may share weather. Temporarily download global inputs on cloud runners, then persist cropped analysis inputs and manifests rather than entire global archives.
    80→    80→
    81→    81→Historical fallback/cross-check: Earth Engine `JAXA/GPM_L3/GSMaP/v8/operational`. Inspect its selected rainfall band and provisional status; retain source identity. It is not interchangeable with IMERG, and it is not an instantaneous local gauge. [R10]
    82→    82→
    83→    83→### Forecasts and ensemble rainfall
    84→    84→
    85→    85→Fetch Open-Meteo hourly from a backend adapter. A tested request is:
    86→    86→
    87→    87→`https://api.open-meteo.com/v1/forecast?latitude=22.36&longitude=91.82&hourly=precipitation&forecast_days=3&timezone=UTC&models=gfs_seamless`
    88→    88→
    89→    89→These coordinates demonstrate regional access, not a verified pilot hill. Store the raw response, requested and returned coordinates, units, fetch time and model/run information when supplied. Precipitation belongs to the **preceding hour**. Request UTC, construct interval start/end explicitly, and display Asia/Dhaka. Calculate next 6/24/48-hour totals separately from past rainfall. [R11]
    90→    90→
    91→    91→Once the core works, integrate **one** documented ensemble family through Open-Meteo. Prefer WeatherNext 2 or ECMWF AIFS if permitted and available; otherwise select a documented conventional ensemble. Verify the exact model identifier and returned member count in a saved response. For each complete member, accumulate the same future 24-hour window; report members exceeding the configured rainfall threshold divided by valid members.
    92→    92→
    93→    93→Show this as **uncalibrated rainfall exceedance probability**, never landslide probability. Do not pool hundreds of members from different models without a validated weighting method. Individual member archives cover only a short recent period; use observed/estimated historical rainfall and a supported Single Runs forecast for July replay, not today's ensemble or hindsight reanalysis. [R12–R13]
    94→    94→
    95→    95→### BMD official warnings
    96→    96→
    97→    97→Discover CAP documents hourly from **both** `https://cap.bmd.gov.bd/api/cap/rss.xml` and the public alert index, following the XML links actually published. Parse CAP 1.2 identifiers, sender, message type, references, sent/effective/onset/expires, text and affected geometry. Store updates/cancellations and deduplicate identifiers/revisions. Restrict manual import to permitted BMD URLs or reviewed uploads.
    98→    98→
    99→    99→The 5 July alert XML retrieved for this plan has effective time **5 July 07:00 UTC** and expiry **6 July 07:00 UTC**, while its text describes 72 hours from 13:00 Bangladesh time on 5 July. Preserve both windows and show **validity conflict — human review required**. Do not silently extend the official XML expiry. A text-derived window is explicitly labeled and reviewed. CAP polygon pairs are latitude/longitude; convert to GeoJSON longitude/latitude and validate point-in-polygon matching. [R14]
   100→   100→
   101→   101→The audit reported an RSS lastBuildDate of 17 August; direct retrieval for this plan returned **29 September 2026**. Keep dual discovery as a resilience measure, but do not claim the feed is currently stuck in August. BMD's lack of active alerts does not establish safety. Publisher instructions stay separate from our screening recommendations.

   103→   103→## 5 Evidence integrity and data freshness
   104→   104→
   105→   105→Every stored input needs provider/product/version, asset ID, valid interval/acquisition dates, issue/run time if known, retrieval time, grid/geometry, units, QA status, file path and SHA-256. Record unknown timestamps as null with a reason.
   106→   106→
   107→   107→Let D be the end of the latest contiguous valid received rainfall interval. Compute complete past 24/48/72-hour and five-day totals ending at D; display D prominently. A total with missing intervals is incomplete. Never fill missing rain with zero, add overlapping accumulations, or merge satellite estimates and forecasts without an explicit validated method. For half-hour inputs, complete windows require the expected 48/96/144/240 intervals.
   108→   108→
   109→   109→Proposed engineering freshness policies: forecast stale after two hours, no new automatic forecast suggestions after six; rainfall delayed when D is over six hours old; optical evidence stale after 14 days; BMD status unknown after two missed hourly checks. These are configurable service policies, not hazard thresholds. Keep official warnings visible even when other feeds fail.
   110→   110→
   111→   111→Use fixed raster alignment, nodata handling and documented resampling. Exchange geometry uses WGS84; Chattogram-area measurement uses an appropriate projected CRS such as EPSG:32646 after checking the final extent. Web Mercator is display-only. Preserve contributing dates in composites and export bytes rather than relying on expiring image tiles.
   112→   112→
   113→   113→Every source has an access state: **live, stale, historical replay or unavailable**. A service-health indicator never implies a hill is safe. UI real-time subscriptions update saved records; they do not make satellite acquisition or rainfall publication instantaneous.

   115→   115→## 6 Analysis and visible AI
   116→   116→
   117→   117→### CutWatch disturbance pipeline
   118→   118→
   119→   119→1. Select comparable short before/after windows or same-season comparisons. Reject badly registered pairs and large seasonal mismatches.
   120→   120→2. Apply cloud/shadow QA and require sufficient **common** valid coverage. Start development with 60% coverage, then tune on development locations.
   121→   121→3. Compute NDVI and documented bare-soil-index change; add Dynamic World probability changes where corresponding data exist.
   122→   122→4. Restrict interpretation to verified hill areas; show DEM slope/relief as context. Address terrain illumination and shadow effects.
   123→   123→5. Group candidate pixels into polygons. An initial 0.1 ha filter is a tunable heuristic, not a proven detection limit; measure misses below that size.
   124→   124→6. Store suspected disturbance with date interval, approximate area, source IDs, valid coverage, method version and uncertainty. Distinguish vegetation clearance, construction, seasonal effects and landslide scars during review.
   125→   125→
   126→   126→Freeze all thresholds before held-out testing. If Dynamic World is missing, label the result as the spectral baseline rather than claiming AI corroboration. The pretrained land-cover and cloud-quality products make AI visible without requiring a large local training run.
   127→   127→
   128→   128→### Weather screening and SlopeRisk
   129→   129→
   130→   130→| Suggested state | Prototype rule | Human action |
   131→   131→| --- | --- | --- |
   132→   132→| Insufficient data | Required imagery/weather quality fails | Inspect source gaps; keep applicable BMD information visible |
   133→   133→| Monitor | No current configured review trigger, with adequate inputs | Continue collection; this is not a safety declaration |
   134→   134→| Review | Suspected disturbance, applicable warning, or documented rainfall comparison crossed | Review evidence and request field checks |
   135→   135→| Priority review suggested | Reviewed configurable rule identifies elevated attention | Authorized reviewer confirms or rejects, with reasons |
   136→   136→
   137→   137→Keep an independent BMD banner in every state. A rainfall crossing does not establish cutting; a suspected cut does not become a legal conclusion or automatically reduce a rainfall threshold.
   138→   138→
   139→   139→Use YPSA's published **100–120 mm/24-hour** trigger as a sourced research comparison, with its local validation context. BMD's heavy/very-heavy terminology describes rainfall categories, not site-level landslide probability. Station thresholds do not transfer automatically to a 10 km satellite cell. Obtain gauge data and expert review before operational calibration. [R14–R15]
   140→   140→
   141→   141→The audit also proposes GSB's 100 mm/3-hour, 200 mm/24-hour and 350 mm/72-hour values, Ahmed et al.'s five-day bands and older 24/48-hour rules. Preserve them in the comparison register, verify their primary definitions and endpoints, then enable them individually. Do not make an unverified combination the production rule. [R16]
   142→   142→
   143→   143→### Measured model and summaries
   144→   144→
   145→   145→Target 20–40 reviewed disturbance examples and 20–40 difficult negatives, grouped by hill/event. If suitable labels exist by 11 October, compare a small Random Forest or gradient-boosted disturbance classifier with the baseline. It replaces the baseline only after a useful held-out comparison; no pixel from the same event may leak across splits.
   146→   146→
   147→   147→Always provide deterministic English and reviewed Bangla summaries. An optional LLM may draft from the stored fact JSON only, with sentence-to-field references, numeric/entity validation and reviewer approval. Provider failure or unsupported output falls back to templates. No external AI API is assumed permitted or free.
   148→   148→
   149→   149→Future calibrated landslide modeling requires a defined site/horizon, adequate timed events, representative non-events, spatial/time splits and separate calibration. A three-hour refresh is not a three-hour prediction horizon. Prithvi's optical-band compatibility, Landslide4Sense's label/task mismatch and TerraTorch/checkpoint versions require a separate research gate; radar cannot simply occupy optical input channels.

   161→   161→
   162→   162→Use foreign keys, spatial/time indexes and unique provider keys. Apply schema migrations from `supabase/migrations/`; regenerate TypeScript types. Store rasters in private buckets, not database rows.
   163→   163→
   164→   164→Read permitted records with the Supabase SDK under row-level security. Sensitive case transitions use a validated database function or Edge Function that checks the user's JWT and role. Restrict callable database functions, fix their search path when using elevated privileges, and validate geometry/payload limits. Users cannot change their own roles.
   165→   165→
   166→   166→Implement thin functions named `refresh-weather`, `discover-bmd`, `dispatch-job`, `review-case`, `request-report`, `get-download` and `source-health`.    167→   167→
   168→   168→Cases move through **candidate, under review, rejected or field verification requested, reviewed, and optionally field corroborated**. Report jobs move through queued, running, completed or failed. Reviews append history and check the expected case revision so concurrent edits cannot silently overwrite a decision. Report creation pins that revision.
   169→   169→
   170→   170→Build map/dashboard, site detail, case review, report archive, and source/job health views.    172→   172→## 8 Report access and safe use
   173→   173→
   174→   174→The report is **Hill disturbance and weather assessment**, marked draft, reviewed or field corroborated. Include case/site/polygon, image dates and QA, approximate changed area, terrain limits, rainfall windows/units/cutoff, forecast horizon, BMD source/status/conflicts, permit/jurisdiction status, reviewer/rationale, next action and input hashes.
   175→   175→
   176→   176→Render print-ready HTML with a bundled Noto Sans Bengali font and tested Bangla conjuncts. Browser printing gives an immediate preview; a GitHub Actions worker uses a shaping-capable Chromium/Playwright renderer to archive the same frozen snapshot as a PDF. Do not generate heavyweight PDFs in Edge Functions. Store report/template versions; refreshing data cannot alter an old report.
   177→   177→
   178→   178→A QR code opens the **authorized evidence snapshot**, not a public case dump. A hash helps detect changed bytes; it does not prove a source claim is true. Preserve lineage so a reviewer can recompute totals and inspect original evidence. Do not claim a legal digital signature or courtroom admissibility from an account approval alone.
   179→   179→
   180→   180→Section 6B enforcement remains with authorized officials, including clearance/permit checks. Separate preparedness from enforcement and household targeting. Display decision-support status and preserve publisher wording without automatic downgrade. No public resident registry, automated summons, eviction instruction or operational voice broadcast is included. [R17]
   181→   181→
   182→   182→The optional finale voice test uses only consented allowlisted numbers, an approved Bangla/Chittagonian test template, deduplication and provider status. Do not expose numbers in logs/reports. CAP export is a future interoperability artifact, not a claimed 1090 or government broadcast integration. Confirm applicable privacy obligations before introducing contacts; the audit's uncertain 2026 legal-status claim is not presented as verified law.

   246→   246→## 11 Evaluation and release acceptance
   247→   247→
   248→   248→Group labels by location/event, tune only on development groups and hold out locations/time where possible. Historical replay admits only inputs demonstrably available by each simulated decision time. When publication time is unknown, disclose the assumption; later imagery cannot establish a pre-event warning.
   249→   249→
   250→   250→Report true detections, false detections, missed disturbances, sample denominators, coverage gaps and a false-positive gallery.
